The computational core of the use-case catalogue is the implicit Multilinear Time-Invariant (iMTI) and tensor formulation developed within the TenSyGrid project~\cite{Kaufmann2025TenSyGridD11,Wong2025TenSyGridD21}. Originally researched and prototyped as a dedicated MTI Toolbox in MATLAB, its algorithms and tensor routines have been ported to Python and integrated into the open-source VeraGrid platform as the \texttt{TenSyGridEngine}.

The formulation represents the time-invariant component models in multilinear form~\cite{deJuanVela2026TenSyGridD33}. The complete system of equations is then transformed following~\cite{Kaufmann2025TenSyGridD11,Wong2025TenSyGridD21} into a tensor representation whose contractions can accelerate Jacobian-matrix computation~\cite{kaufmann2026small}.

The dependency on this engine differs by use case. UC-01, UC-02, UC-03, and UC-05 directly consume iMTI-based eigenvalues, eigenvectors, participation factors, or repeated post-contingency linearisations. UC-04 is a complementary power-flow voltage check included to keep the operating-point assessment coherent, but it is not itself an iMTI calculation. The proposed analytical use cases build on the same engine to different degrees, whereas UC-12 packages results produced by the other use cases into a report. These distinctions are made explicit in the descriptions and workflow maps below.

\section{Candidate workflows outside the current implementation scope}
During the use-case identification process, several potential workflows were considered but not included in the current implementation plan. The main reasons were that a workflow requires data or infrastructure that is not available within the project, addresses a different stability phenomenon, or depends on research that must mature before it can be specified as an implementable use case.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Full real-time operational integration.} The online variant of UC-08 proposes tracking stability margins at a conceptual level. A production deployment with continuous SCADA or PMU feeds, streaming state estimation, cybersecurity controls, and periodic re-linearisation requires operational infrastructure that is outside the scope of D4.1.

  \item \textbf{Exhaustive time-domain transient-stability simulation.} Large-disturbance stability assessment remains represented by UC-14, consistently with the project proposal. UC-14 computes the stability-region boundary geometrically, using the stable manifold of the controlling unstable equilibrium and the associated heteroclinic orbits obtained from the multilinear representation. Exhaustive EMT or RMS sweeps require additional modelling assumptions, computational infrastructure, and validation metrics. Full time-domain validation of the most severe contingencies is therefore recorded as a possible future extension that would complement, rather than replace, UC-14.

  \item \textbf{Optimal power flow with stability constraints.} Integrating stability constraints directly into an optimal power flow formulation would allow the toolbox to suggest generation re-dispatches or other control actions that guarantee a minimum stability margin. This involves solving a non-convex, constrained optimisation problem and is considered a longer-term research direction.

  \item \textbf{Automated control parameter tuning.} While UC-10 proposes to suggest \textit{where} to place damping controllers, automatically computing the optimal PSS or FACTS parameters (gain, time constants) requires solving a control-design optimisation problem that is beyond the scope of the current deliverable.

  \item \textbf{Multi-area oscillation and inter-area mode analysis.} Identifying and characterising inter-area modes involving geographically separated groups of generators, and computing metrics such as mode shape, observability, and controllability, is partially addressed by UC-03 (participation factors). However, a dedicated inter-area-mode tool with geographical visualisation of mode shapes would require additional development and is proposed as a candidate for operator feedback.
\end{itemize}

\section{Organization}

The proposed use cases answer two different kinds of questions: the current system status and its evolution along monitored operating trajectories, and planning studies over longer horizons. Because these questions call for different workflows, the toolbox is organised around a small number of tabs, each grouping the use cases that share a purpose:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Run analysis.} UC-01: execute the small-signal stability computation that feeds the rest of the views.
  \item \textbf{Grid information.} UC-04: inspect the steady-state voltage profile of the loaded model.
  \item \textbf{Status and monitoring.} UC-02, UC-03, UC-07, the online variant of UC-08, UC-13 and UC-14: stability verdict and eigenvalue spectrum, critical modes, device-level participation, online trajectory monitoring, and the centre-manifold and region-of-attraction analyses.
  \item \textbf{Planning.} UC-05, UC-06, UC-08, UC-09, UC-10 and UC-11: N-1 contingencies, scenario comparison, trend analysis, critical-element ranking, damping-controller placement, and renewable-penetration assessment.
  \item \textbf{Queue and reports.} UC-12: batch execution and automated report generation.
\end{itemize}

This grouping is indicative: the exact distribution of the tabs will be refined with the feedback of colleagues and our experience while implementing it.
